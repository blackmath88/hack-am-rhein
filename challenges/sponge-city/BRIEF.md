# Make Basel a Sponge

> Teach Basel to soak up the rain and sweat out the heat.

**Domain:** architecture

## Original challenge wording

Basel's summers are getting hotter and its storms heavier. The city has a map for each problem, but nobody has built on them together. Pick a place, take the region's open data, and create something that helps: a tool, a design, a map, a story.

## The situation

Heatwaves in Switzerland are three times as frequent as at the start of the 20th century, and heat is now the natural hazard that kills the most people in the country: several hundred a year, and around 1,400 in the summer of 2003. Basel is already about 2°C warmer than in pre-industrial times, and without climate protection that keeps rising.

You can see it in the city. Beeches and maples struggle through hot, dry summers. In the Rhine, trout and grayling come under stress once the water passes 20°C.

The same shift brings heavier, shorter bursts of rain. In August 2022 a single storm flooded cellars across Riehen, where the drainage was designed for a rain event that comes once every five years. Basel's sewers were sized for a rainfall pattern that no longer exists.

On paper, the canton has answers. A climate analysis from 2019 models heat street by street, and the Stadtklimakonzept built on it is binding for planning and building permits. The sponge city principle (Schwammstadt) commits Basel to letting rainwater soak in, evaporate or be stored close to where it falls, instead of rushing it into the sewer. Nationally, FOEN's surface runoff hazard map shows where heavy rain floods the ground, using one method for the whole country.

## Why it matters

Both maps exist. Different offices read them, against different standards. A square or street planned today gets checked for heat and for water as if they were two unrelated problems. Yet the places where both risks peak are often exactly where one well-chosen change could do both jobs. Nobody has pointed at a map yet and said: here.

The people paying the most for this are not at the planning table. Residents vulnerable to heat, street trees that are struggling, a sewer network built for yesterday's storms.

## Your task

Create something that helps Basel handle heat, heavy rain, or both. Build it on the region's own data.

The form is up to you. A tool a planner would open on Monday morning. A redesign of one square that shows what a sponge city looks like up close. A map that makes an invisible risk impossible to overlook. An app, a game, an installation, a story that reaches the people most affected. Any of these can be the strongest answer in the room.

You don't have to weigh heat and water equally. Going deep on one is just as valid as connecting both. What counts is that it's built on real data about real places, and that it makes a point you can defend.

## Choose your scale

- **Micro:** one place. A square, a courtyard, a building, a single street.
- **Meso:** a street, a block, a neighbourhood.
- **Macro:** a district, a municipality or the whole city.

Your scale sets the level of detail. A city-wide concept is judged on the strength of its argument, not on whether every street holds up. A single square should go deeper, but shouldn't claim to speak for the whole city.

Gaps are normal at every scale. Basel is not uniform, and a solution that claims to work everywhere is usually claiming too much. Pick the scale where your idea is strongest, not the one that looks most ambitious.

If part of your idea would also work somewhere else, that's a real plus. Showing what transfers and what doesn't is valuable knowledge in itself. But it's a bonus on top of a concrete answer for this region, not a replacement for one.

## Data

The resources below are your starting point. Your area doesn't have to stop at the canton border: data for Basel-Stadt, Basel-Landschaft and the whole of Switzerland is listed. Keep in mind that the two cantons' climate analyses use different future years (2030 and 2035).

You're welcome to bring in other open data, such as precipitation return period files, community datasets from Kaggle or other regional sources. Just state the licence and origin on your sources slide.
