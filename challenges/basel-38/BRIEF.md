# Too hot to handle — Basel at 38

> Heat is the quietest emergency in the city. Help people get through it.

**Domain:** social-impact

## Original challenge wording

Heat affects more people in Switzerland than any other natural hazard, and it hits the most vulnerable first. Basel knows exactly where it gets hottest. Now connect that knowledge to the people who need it on the day.

## The situation

Between June and September 2026, around 600 more people died in Switzerland than in a normal summer. The first heatwave, at the end of June, hit hardest, because bodies had not yet adapted to the heat.

Heat does not hit everyone the same way. Older people, small children, pregnant women and people with chronic illness are most at risk. So are people who live alone, who cannot easily leave their flat, or who do not speak the language the warning came in. Many of them live in the densest quarters, in flats that never cool down at night.

Basel is better prepared than most. It is one of only eight cantons with a heat action plan that meets international standards. When MeteoSwiss issues a level 3 warning, extra measures kick in. There is a heat hotline for older people, run with Pro Senectute. There is heat advice recorded in 14 languages. There is a list of cool public rooms where anyone can sit without buying anything, and more than 200 public fountains.

## Why it matters

The help exists. Getting it to the right person at the right moment is the hard part.

The list of cool rooms is a table on a web page. The hotline runs on weekdays during office hours. The warnings go out through channels the most vulnerable people often do not use. And the people who visit them, from home care teams to neighbours, start every heatwave morning with the same question: who first?

Nobody yet connects the forecast, the places to cool down, the way to get there and the people who need them most.

## Your task

Create something that helps people in Basel stay safe and well during a heatwave. Build it on the region's own data.

The form is up to you. A route that gets someone to a cool room without collapsing on the way. A service that turns a heat warning into a phone call, a text, or a note under the door. A tool that helps a care team plan their morning. A map of the places where there is nowhere to go. An app, a game, a campaign, an installation. Any of these can be the strongest answer in the room.

What counts is that it helps a real person on a real hot day, and that you can show who that person is.

## Choose who you build for

- **One person:** someone at risk, trying to get through the day.
- **The helpers:** care teams, hotline staff, neighbours, volunteers.
- **The city:** the people deciding where help should go next summer.

Your choice sets the level of detail. A tool for one person should be simple enough to use on a bad day. A tool for the city is judged on how well it argues where help is missing.

Be honest about who you leave out. Many people at risk do not own a smartphone. A solution that assumes they do is solving a different problem.

Health data is personal. Work with aggregated data only, and never build something that points at a single person or household.

## Data

The resources below are your starting point: the city's heat maps, temperature sensors, fountains, trees, population by quarter, and the canton's own heat and health information. Swiss data comes in LV95, most web maps want WGS84. Convert once, when you load it.

You are welcome to bring in other open data, such as OpenStreetMap or public transport data. Just state the licence and origin on your sources slide.
