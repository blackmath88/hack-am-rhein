# Brainstorm — Too hot to handle: Basel at 38

## Problem beneath the brief

Basel already has substantial information and services. The deeper problem may be:

> **How do we align the forces around the cause so existing capacity produces the best possible result?**

This is not only a routing or warning problem. It may be a **change / coordination journey around a public cause**.

## Cause

> Reduce preventable heat harm in Basel during extreme heat events.

## Broken decision

> When a dangerous heat day begins, where should limited human effort and existing services go first, and which actors need to align to make that action possible?

## Cause-centred stakeholder mapping

Instead of putting a project or institution in the centre, put the cause in the centre.

Possible flow:

```
CAUSE
Reduce preventable heat harm
↓
ACTORS
Who can affect the cause?
↓
FORCES
What helps or blocks it right now?
↓
DEPENDENCIES
Who needs whom?
↓
LEVERAGE
Which coordination changes produce the largest effect?
↓
MINIMUM ALIGNMENT
What can actors commit to without full consensus?
↓
ACTION
What happens today?
```

Potential actors:

- Basel-Stadt
- MeteoSwiss
- Pro Senectute
- Spitex / home-care teams
- cool-room operators
- pharmacies
- public transport
- neighbours
- volunteers
- NGOs
- municipal services

## Deciding without agreeing

Working principle:

> Alignment does not require identical perspectives.

Different actors can retain different mandates and motivations while still making compatible commitments around the cause.

Potential output:

```
WHEN
Level 3 heat warning

PRIORITY AREA
[area derived from aggregated evidence]

MINIMUM ALIGNMENT
City → priority picture
Care teams → reprioritise visits
Hotline / outreach → target communication
Cool rooms → confirm availability
Volunteers / neighbours → cover gaps

UNRESOLVED
What remains disputed or unknown stays visible.
```

## Making information actionable

```
INFORMATION
"We know where it is hot."
↓
MEANING
"This area combines heat + vulnerability + poor cooling access."
↓
STAKEHOLDER REALITY
"These actors can change the situation."
↓
ALIGNMENT
"These actions can happen without changing their mandates."
↓
ACTION
"Do these things this morning."
```

## What AI might be for

Not authority.

Potentially:

- interpret actor roles and constraints;
- maintain a changing force map;
- identify dependencies and gaps;
- suggest minimum viable alignment;
- translate fragmented information into action candidates;
- explain why an action may help the cause;
- preserve visible tensions instead of manufacturing consensus.

## What should stay explicit / deterministic

- official warnings;
- source datasets;
- aggregated population data;
- service locations / opening information;
- privacy boundary;
- accepted commitments;
- final prioritisation rules where consequential;
- provenance.

## Relationship to existing work

This connects the GIS / evidence work with the change-management side:

- spatial data tells us **where the pressure is**;
- stakeholder / force mapping tells us **what can actually move**.

## Open questions

- Is the primary user the city, a coordination team, care teams, or a multi-actor room?
- Can we model forces without pretending to know stakeholder intent?
- What is the smallest useful “minimum alignment” case?
- How do we distinguish evidence from AI interpretation?
- Can the system show disagreement explicitly rather than score it away?
