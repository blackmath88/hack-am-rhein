# Cross-cutting ideas

These are not challenge requirements. They are working ideas that may illuminate more than one challenge.

## The gap between information and agency

Across the four challenges, the missing thing often does not appear to be more technology or more data.

A recurring shape is:

```
INFORMATION
↓
MEANING
↓
DECISION
↓
COORDINATION
↓
ACTION
```

The useful product may be the intermediate object that makes the next decision possible.

## Making information actionable

Do not stop at:

- map,
- dataset,
- search result,
- alert,
- recommendation,
- dashboard.

Ask instead:

- What decision does this information change?
- Who is allowed or able to act?
- What must be true before action is justified?
- What evidence should travel with the action?
- What is still unknown?
- What needs coordination rather than more analysis?

## Deciding without agreeing

Many public and organisational problems involve actors with different mandates, incentives and interpretations.

Alignment does not necessarily require consensus.

A system may instead help identify:

- a shared cause,
- relevant actors,
- forces helping or blocking the cause,
- dependencies,
- minimum viable commitments,
- actions that can proceed despite different motivations,
- unresolved tensions that should remain visible.

## Cause-centred stakeholder mapping

Classic stakeholder maps often describe actors around a project.

An alternative:

> Put the **cause** at the centre and map actors and forces by how they can move the cause.

Possible flow:

```
CAUSE
↓
ACTORS
↓
FORCES
↓
DEPENDENCIES
↓
LEVERAGE
↓
MINIMUM ALIGNMENT
↓
ACTION
```

AI could help maintain the semantic interpretation of this changing system while preserving source evidence and human authority.

## AI role

A useful default:

- AI interprets ambiguity.
- Deterministic systems retain source facts, constraints and explicit rules.
- Humans own commitments and consequential decisions.
- Provenance stays visible.
